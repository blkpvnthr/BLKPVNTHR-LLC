STRIX

Structured Traceability, Retrieval, and Intelligence eXtraction.

STRIX is a research-stage computational intelligence platform investigating methods for generating independently verifiable, temporally relevant, evidence-supported, and reproducible conclusions. Everything on this page describes research objectives and technical uncertainties, not validated performance.

Research status

STRIX is under active research and development. The capabilities described here are research objectives and technical hypotheses. They have not been independently validated, and no performance, funding, patent, or certification claims are made or implied.

01 Research problem

Plausible is not the same as supported.

Contemporary AI systems can generate plausible output without demonstrating that their assertions are sufficiently supported, current, independently verifiable, or appropriate for a particular institutional context. Existing AI-enabled research systems can exhibit unsupported assertions, outdated information, conflicting evidence, incomplete provenance, and insufficient independent verification.

02 Research objective

Integrate evidence, time, and verification into one process.

Investigate computational methods for integrating evidence quality, temporal validity, provenance, contradiction analysis, uncertainty, and independent verification into a single, coherent research and decision-support process — including principled abstention when support is insufficient.

03 Research questions

Open questions and technical uncertainties.

The research investigates whether the proposed methodology can achieve the following. These are questions under investigation, not established breakthroughs.

Q1

Can it improve assertion-level factual reliability?

Q2

Can it improve the temporal relevance of retrieved evidence?

Q3

Can it identify unsupported conclusions?

Q4

Can it detect conflicting evidence?

Q5

Can it improve appropriate abstention?

Q6

Can it produce reproducible research artifacts?

Q7

Can it preserve institutional knowledge boundaries?

Q8

Can it remain computationally practical at institutional scale?

04 Conceptual architecture

From a question to a reproducible record.

A conceptual representation of the research process under investigation. It illustrates the intended flow of information, not an operational system or measured behavior.

  1. Step 01 Input

    Research question

    A question posed within a defined institutional knowledge context.

  2. Step 02 Retrieval

    Evidence discovery

    Candidate evidence is gathered from governed sources.

  3. Step 03 Assessment

    Source & temporal assessment

    Each source is assessed for quality and temporal validity.

  4. Step 04 Drafting

    Candidate assertions

    Draft assertions are formed and linked to their evidence.

  5. Step 05 Verification

    Independent verification

    Assertions are checked independently of the drafting step.

  6. Step 06 Analysis

    Contradiction & uncertainty

    Conflicts and uncertainty are identified and characterized.

  7. Step 07 Decision

    Supported conclusion or abstention

    A conclusion is issued only when adequately supported; otherwise it abstains.

  8. Step 08 Record

    Reproducible research record

    The full basis is retained so the result can be reproduced and reviewed.

05 Proposed technical validation

How the research would be evaluated.

The research proposes comparative evaluations against conventional retrieval-augmented generation, citation-enabled AI research systems, and other appropriate baselines. Quantitative results will be published only when independently supported by documented experiments.

Evaluation dimensions

Reliability

  • Assertion accuracy
  • Citation correctness
  • Unsupported-assertion detection
  • Contradiction detection

Evaluation dimensions

Judgment

  • Temporal validity
  • Abstention quality
  • Research reproducibility
  • Knowledge-boundary enforcement

Evaluation dimensions

Practicality

  • Latency
  • Computational resource utilization

06 Institutional demonstration

Proposed evaluation across governed environments.

The research proposes evaluating STRIX across independently governed knowledge environments, to test whether boundaries can be preserved while the methodology holds up in different domains.

01

Engineering & project management

Technical and programmatic knowledge, kept within its own boundary.

02

Software engineering & cybersecurity

Code, configuration, and security knowledge, independently governed.

03

Scientific & technical research

Literature and experimental knowledge, with provenance and timing.

Scope of demonstration material

Demonstrations may use authorized public documents and synthetic institutional records. Use of any organization's public materials does not imply that organization's participation, sponsorship, or endorsement.
